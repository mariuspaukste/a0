How the economy works

Each December a Leontief input–output model is run for every state. Column j below is what sector j buys from each sector to make one unit of output. Gross output is x = (I − A)⁻¹ d, where d is what households eat and wear plus cloth bought for export. GDP is the value added. The multiplier is how much the whole economy produces for one more unit of a sector's goods.

Farming can't produce more than the year's harvest in each town. When it falls short, households cut back on cloth and goods more than on food, so a failed monsoon also reaches the weavers. A state the Company rules pays part of its spending as revenue, which the Company spends on cloth to ship home.

The coefficients are round, illustrative numbers, not estimates for a real year. Totals are scaled so 1600 matches Angus Maddison's estimates for India (135 million people, $74 billion in 1990 dollars), and his figures are shown for comparison.
